On September 24th, community.lsst.org will go into read-only mode for the day because major changes are afoot!

**When work is complete,  the forum will be available at a new domain: rubin.community**

Click below if you're interested in the gory details. 

[details="Gory details"]

* We are currently self-hosting this forum, which is based on the Discourse platform. On the 24th we are changing to an infrastructure hosted by Discourse. * The dedicated new domain for the forum (rubin.community) improves our ability to support the forum and add new capabilities (and is a better match for how we refer to our community since the name change to Rubin Observatory). 

* We'll be putting mechanisms in place to make sure that folks with links/bookmarks to community.lsst.org will be re-directed to the new location until we're sure everyone has gotten the message. 

* Your account, history and posts will all be migrated to the new forum. Nothing will be lost. 

* We expect migration and checkout to take most of the day, so plan around that. Content on community.lsst.org will be available throughout this time, but you will not be able to post new content on migration day to avoid losing any content in the migration.

* Updates on the migration and announcement that the work is complete will also be posted in banners on the Rubin Science Platform at [data.lsst.cloud](https://data.lsst.cloud)

This sounds like a great plan. Thanks for working on it.

But re:

> We’ll be putting mechanisms in place to make sure that folks with links/bookmarks to community.lsst.org 1 will be re-directed to the new location until we’re sure everyone has gotten the message.

I think that there is no point in time at which everyone will have effectively *gotten the message*. The site has existed for quite some time and is very public and lots of people have saved links In lots of ways to lots of very interesting and historic information already. See the classic post on this topic from 1998 by the inventor of the World Wide Web, Tim Berners-Lee

[Cool URIs don't change](https://www.w3.org/Provider/Style/URI)

The whole thing is a great read, but this section is worth quoting here: 

> Why should I care?
> When you change a URI on your server, you can never completely tell who will have links to the old URI. They might have made links from regular web pages. They might have bookmarked your page. They might have scrawled the URI in the margin of a letter to a friend.

> When someone follows a link and it breaks, they generally lose confidence in the owner of the server. They also are frustrated - emotionally and practically from accomplishing their goal.

> Enough people complain all the time about dangling links that I hope the damage is obvious. I hope it also obvious that the reputation damage is to the maintainer of the server whose document vanished.

So please keep the old domain active for the indefinite future with redirect links to the content on the new site! I trust that the good folks running Discourse have ways to make that straightforward.
