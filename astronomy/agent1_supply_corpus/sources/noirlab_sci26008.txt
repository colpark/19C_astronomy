NSF–DOE Rubin Observatory Celebrates First Public Alerts
February 25, 2026
The first batch of world-public alerts generated by NSF–DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory arrived successfully at Rubin’s community brokers on the night of 24 February 2026. Now available to anyone in the world via Rubin’s community broker platforms, these first alerts included tens of thousands of moving and changing objects in the sky, including asteroids, variable stars, supernovae, and active galactic nuclei. A press release celebrating the achievement was issued on 25 February 2026.
Rubin’s alert system will eventually produce up to seven million alerts per night, and about 20 billion alerts in total over the ten-year Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST). Rubin’s alerts are public to the world, meaning anyone — from professional researchers to students and citizen scientists — can access and explore them through any of seven community brokers, as well as two downstream services. These services form an international network enabling prompt data exploration and collaborative scientific research. Additionally, through collaborations with platforms like Zooniverse, Rubin is empowering the global community to help classify cosmic events and contribute directly to discovery.
